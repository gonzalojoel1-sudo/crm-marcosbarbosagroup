# Agenda/list views that are fully keyboard-operable — how the top calendar products do it

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Scope:** the CRM's **List** (agenda) view: create, edit, move/reschedule, change duration, duplicate, delete — without a mouse.
**Companion docs:** `2026-09-16-calendario-ux-competidores.md`, `2026-09-16-agenda-hallazgos.md`, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-16-crm-agenda-operar-sin-mouse-design.md` (§4/S3, §7).
**Rule:** every concrete claim carries a source URL. Where no primary source exists, it says **unverified** rather than guessing. Read-only list views are called out as such.

> **Headline finding.** Almost nobody makes the *list* the place you reschedule from. Move/resize is a **grid** affordance everywhere (drag), and where a list supports movement at all it is via a **form dialog** (Outlook's "Move to Date"), not arrows. The list's documented keyboard strength is **create / open / edit / delete**, plus a **command palette**. Duplicate-from-list is essentially undocumented everywhere. The best products push *all* verbs through **one command surface (⌘K) + one per-row menu**, and follow the ARIA APG rule for focus after deletion.

## 2. The 5 strongest patterns worth copying

### P1 — One per-row action surface, reachable by keyboard **and** by click (the "menus" pattern)
Every product with a real keyboard reputation routes verbs through **one command surface**: Google/Superhuman/Notion/Vimcal/Todoist all lean on `⌘K`/`Ctrl+K` with "learn the shortcut on the right", and Google Drive uses a single **"Move to…"** command. The WCAG technique for the "no dragging" requirement is literally this shape — activate the item, open a menu, choose the destination (G219), with the documented failure being Kaizen-board drag (F108). It satisfies **2.1.1 and 2.5.7 with one control**.
→ **Copy:** the spec's S3 menu (Edit · Move to… · Duration · Duplicate · Delete) is well-founded. Open it with `Enter`/`Space` on the focused row, and make every item clickable. Source: [APG keyboard interface](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/) · [W3C 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)

### P2 — The list is a **composite widget**: arrows move focus inside, Tab enters/leaves once (roving tabindex)
The APG is unambiguous: a composite (grid, listbox, tree) **includes only one of its focusable elements in the page tab sequence**, and authors **must manage focus movement inside it**; the two sanctioned techniques are **roving `tabindex`** and **`aria-activedescendant`**. Google's Schedule view implements exactly this: `←/→` move between days, `↑/↓` between events in a day. Todoist uses `J/K`/`↑↓`. This is the opposite of "tab through every event button".
→ **Copy:** roving tabindex across rows (not `tabindex=0` on every event), with arrows for days↔events. It also directly reduces the tab-stop count that makes a long agenda unusable. Source: [APG keyboard interface — roving tabindex](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/) · [APG Grid](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/grid/) · [Google 6101541](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/6101541)

### P3 — "Move to…" is a **form dialog** (date picker + time field), not arrows
No product documents arrow-nudging **from a list**. Nudging is a grid affordance (Fantastical: `⌃⌘←/→`, `⌃⌘↑/↓` — documented only "when selecting an item in Day or Week view"). Where a list/agenda does reschedule, it is a dialog: Outlook's right-click **Move to Date → "Move Appointment"** with a date picker and a time dropdown; Google and Apple iOS open the event form and change the date/time fields.
→ **Copy:** the spec's "Mover a…" dialog is the industry shape. Build it as the **APG Date Picker Dialog** (text field + "choose" button), keep the grid visible behind it (non-modal), and keep the arrow-nudge as a bonus, not the only path. Source: [APG Date Picker Dialog](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/examples/datepicker-dialog/) · [Outlook Move to Date (secondary)](https://wisechecker.com/move-outlook-calendar-event-without-opening/) · [Fantastical shortcuts](https://flexibits.com/fantastical/help/keyboard-shortcuts)

### P4 — Focus after a destructive action has a **written rule**: land on the neighbour
The APG states it outright: if the user "performs a destructive operation like **deleting an item from a list**, the active element may be hidden or removed… set focus on… **the list item following the deleted item**". It also warns that otherwise the browser drops focus to `<body>`. Microsoft's new-Outlook work follows the same philosophy — after launch or search, "the default focus is directly on the first item in the message list instead of the entire list region", explicitly to minimise navigation. Delete-without-confirm is the norm (Google `Delete`, Outlook `Delete`), with **undo** (`z` in Google) rather than a confirmation dialog.
→ **Copy:** after delete, focus the next row (previous if it was last); after create, focus the new row; after cancel, restore focus to the trigger. Reconsider the spec's confirm-on-delete — the market uses undo, not confirmation. Source: [APG keyboard interface — persistence of focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/) · [Microsoft techcommunity](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/outlook/accessibility-and-usability-improvements-in-the-new-outlook-for-windows/4230111)

### P5 — Announce **once**: choose focus OR live region, never both; keep the announcer node alive
No calendar product publishes guidance, but the web-a11y sources are explicit and agree with the spec's §7:
- The WICG `ariaNotify` explainer names the exact failure: using notifications "for scenarios that are already handled by screen readers (such as for **focus-change actions**) result[s] in confusing **double-announcements**".
- Practical guidance: "Do **not** move focus… Announce through the live region and leave focus exactly where it is" — *or*, when results are reached by an explicit action, moving focus is defensible **and then say what happened** — never both.
- The region must exist **empty at mount** (adding the node and its text in the same tick drops the first message) and must **not be re-created on render** (JAWS re-announces a re-inserted region).
→ **Copy:** the spec's "annunciador persistente fuera del subárbol, foco y región viva alternativos" is exactly right. Source: [WICG accessible-notifications](https://github.com/WICG/accessible-notifications) · [client-side-form](https://www.client-side-form.com/accessibility-and-error-ux/aria-live-regions-for-form-errors/) · [MFA11y](https://modern-framework-accessibility.com/react-nextjs-accessibility-patterns/accessible-search-and-filtering/announcing-search-result-counts-to-screen-readers/)

**Bonus (worth stealing):** Outlook's **`F2` inline cell edit** in the List view — rename or change the time of the focused column without leaving the row ([NVDA #11151](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/issues/11151)). And the **per-day "Add task"** in Todoist's Upcoming is the exact precedent for the spec's per-day "Nueva reunión" ([Todoist Upcoming](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/plan-your-week-with-the-upcoming-view-OKOg1mR8)).

## 3. What is missing / worse everywhere (what **not** to copy)

1. **Move/resize from the list is the universal gap.** Fantastical documents `⌃⌘`/`⌃⇧` nudges **only for Day/Week grid selections**; Google and Apple iOS make you open a form; Todoist/Notion/Amie/Reclaim make you **drag**. **No product documents a keyboard-only reschedule *from the list*.** The "Move to…" dialog is the best available shape and even that is only documented for classic Outlook, via right-click. → Being able to move from the list is a genuine differentiator, not a catch-up.
2. **Duplicate-from-list is undocumented almost everywhere.** Only Fantastical mentions duplication, and only by **dragging**; no keyboard duplicate in a list. → Do not look for a model to copy; invent the menu item.
3. **No product publishes list row/section semantics for assistive tech.** Google documents behaviour ("the number of events in a day affects the view") but not DOM; only native Outlook (table + columns) and APG/MDN give structural guidance. So "day heading → list" (with `<h3>`+`<ul>`, per MDN's preference for semantic HTML over `role="list"`) is **our call**, not a copy. Sources: [MDN list role](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Roles/list_role) · [Google 6101541](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/6101541)
4. **"Number of events per day" is never announced.** Google's own wording ("the number of events in a day affects the view") describes **layout**, not an announcement. No product documents announcing a count. → **Unverified everywhere**; if we want it, it is our own addition.
5. **Empty days.** No product documents whether empty days are shown or skipped. Google's "the view may include events for one or more days" is ambiguous → **unverified**.
6. **No calendar ships guidance on the double-announcement problem.** Only the WICG/community sources do. So a documented focus-vs-live-region rule in our spec is ahead of the field.
7. **New Outlook is a cautionary tale of regression.** Microsoft **removed** List and Schedule views from new Outlook ("not available in new Outlook at this time"), and dropped the custom-view/table that made the classic List operable. Source: [MS view doc](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/change-how-you-view-your-outlook-calendar-a4e0dfd2-89a1-4770-9197-a3e786f4cd8f). → An operable list is a maintenance commitment, not a one-off feature; it can be deleted by a redesign.
8. **Confirmation vs undo.** Google and Outlook delete on `Delete` with no confirmation (Google offers `z` undo). The current spec confirms delete — a divergence from every product surveyed. Sources: [Google 37034](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37034) · [Outlook shortcuts](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/keyboard-shortcuts-for-outlook-3cdeb221-7ae5-4c1d-8c1d-9e63216c1efd)
9. **Documentation quality is itself a gap.** Vimcal's docs (Command Center) never enumerate the keys; Notion's official shortcut page just says "press `?`"; Amie shipped an agenda view with **zero** interaction docs. Our spec should enumerate keys in-app (`?`) **and** in docs, per APG's "Ensure Basic Access Via Navigation".
